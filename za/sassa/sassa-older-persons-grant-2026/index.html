SASSA Older Persons Grant 2026: R2,400 Amount, Payment Dates and eLife

SASSA Older Persons Grant 2026: R2,400 a month (R2,420 from age 75), how payment dates work, the 2026 means test, eLife certification, the Postbank card change and how to apply.

By SA Local Info Published 16 May 2026 · Updated 25 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Key points

  • From 1 April 2026: R2,400 a month if you are 60 to 74, and R2,420 from age 75.
  • Older Persons Grants are usually paid first each month, on or just after the 2nd.
  • If SASSA asks you to do eLife certification, do it before the deadline, or your grant may be suspended.

The SASSA Older Persons Grant (also known as the old age grant or pension) increased on 1 April 2026. If you are aged 60 to 74, you now receive R2,400 per month. If you are 75 or older, you receive R2,420 per month. Here is everything you need to know about payment dates, the 2026 means test, eLife certification and how to apply.

Older Persons Grant amount for 2026

The new amounts were published in the Government Gazette in March 2026 and apply from 1 April 2026 until at least March 2027. There is no further increase in October 2026.

Age group2025/26 amountFrom 1 April 2026Increase
Ages 60–74R2,315R2,400+R85
Ages 75 and olderR2,335R2,420+R85

The increase of R85 per month is about 3.7%. If you already get the grant, the higher amount is paid automatically. You do not need to do anything.

When the grant is paid

SASSA announces the payment dates for each month in advance. The Older Persons Grant is paid first, followed by the Disability Grant the next day and children’s grants the day after that.

The usual pattern:

  • Payments start on or just after the 2nd of the month.
  • SASSA does not pay on the 1st, on a Monday, on a weekend, or on the day after a public holiday. If a date falls on one of those days, payment moves to the next suitable day.

For example, Older Persons Grants were paid on 2 April, 5 May and 2 September 2026.

Once the money is paid into your account, you can draw it at any time. You do not have to rush to an ATM or pay point on the first day.

How you receive your money

  • Into your own bank account
  • On a Postbank card at ATMs and retail pay points

Note: Old SASSA Gold Cards stopped working after 31 August 2026 and were replaced by Postbank Black Cards. If you still have a Gold Card, swap it for free at a Postbank replacement site with your SA ID. Dial *120*355# to find one, or call Postbank on 0800 53 54 55. Money already paid to you is not lost.

eLife certification

SASSA uses life certification to confirm that beneficiaries are still alive and still qualify. Since 30 March 2026 you can do it online with eLife on the SASSA Online Services Portal, using a selfie to confirm your identity.

  • SASSA encourages all beneficiaries to complete life certification once a year.
  • If SASSA tells you to certify by a certain date, you must do it before that deadline. If you don’t, your payment can be delayed or your grant suspended.

How to complete it:

  1. Online: go to services.sassa.gov.za and follow the eLife steps. You need your ID number, a cellphone and a phone camera for the selfie.
  2. In person: visit any SASSA office with your green barcoded ID book or Smart ID card.

It is free. Ask a family member to help with the online option if you are not used to smartphones. SASSA will never ask for your bank PIN or for money to certify you.

Who qualifies for the Older Persons Grant

You must meet all of the following:

  • Be 60 years or older
  • Be a South African citizen, permanent resident or recognised refugee, living in South Africa
  • Pass the means test (income and asset limits)
  • Not be cared for in a state-funded institution
  • Not receive another social grant for yourself

Note: If you are married, your spouse’s income and assets are also counted in the means test.

Means test limits (2026)

SingleMarried (combined)
Monthly income limitR9,350R18,700
Yearly income limitR112,200R224,400
Asset limitR1,584,000R3,168,000

These are the limits reported for 2026. SASSA had not published its own updated table when we checked in November 2026, so ask at your SASSA office if your income is close to the limit.

The grant is paid on a sliding scale. If you have some private income below the limit, your grant is reduced. The less private income you have, the more grant you get. If your income or assets are above the limit, you do not qualify.

Documents you need to apply

Take originals and certified copies of:

  • Your 13-digit barcoded ID or Smart ID card, and your spouse’s ID if you are married
  • Proof of marital status: marriage certificate, divorce order or your spouse’s death certificate
  • Your latest payslip (less than three months old), or proof that you have no income
  • Three months of bank statements for all your active accounts
  • A bank confirmation letter for the account you want the grant paid into (in your own name)
  • Proof of deductions such as tax, medical aid or a private pension
  • Proof of assets, such as property you do not live in, shares or investments

If you do not have an ID, go to your SASSA office anyway and ask which other documents they accept.

How to apply

  1. Go to your nearest SASSA office with your documents, or start your application on the SASSA Online Services Portal at services.sassa.gov.za.
  2. Your fingerprints and photo are taken. Biometric enrolment is compulsory for new applications.
  3. Keep your receipt. It is your proof that you applied.
  4. If you do not get an answer on the day, follow up at the office, on the toll-free line or by email.
  5. If you are approved, your grant is paid from the date you applied.

Applying is free. If anyone asks you to pay to apply, report it to SASSA’s fraud line on 0800 701 701.

When the grant stops

Your grant can lapse if you:

  • Leave South Africa for more than 90 days
  • Are admitted to a state-funded institution
  • Do not claim it for 90 days after it was approved

Tell SASSA if your income, marital status or address changes.

Contact SASSA

  • Toll-free: 0800 60 10 11
  • Email: GrantEnquiries@sassa.gov.za
  • Website: www.sassa.gov.za
